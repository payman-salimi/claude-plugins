# Training Standards — Shared Reference

Single source of truth for the numbers every elite-coach skill leans on. When a specialty skill quotes a volume landmark, protein target, RPE definition, or deload prescription, it should match this file. If research or the coach's judgment moves a number, change it here first and then in the skills that repeat it.

Skills read this file when they need to reconcile a number, teach a coach the reasoning behind one (`coach-development`), or when two specialty lenses disagree and `program-design` has to pick.

---

## RPE and reps in reserve (RIR)

RPE is rated per working set, not per session. "I felt tired today" is not RPE 9.

| RPE | Reps left in the tank |
|---|---|
| 10 | 0 — true failure |
| 9 | 1 |
| 8 | 2 |
| 7 | 3 |
| 6 | 4 |
| ≤ 5 | Warm-up territory |

A **hard set** is one taken to RPE 7 or higher (3 or fewer reps in reserve). Volume landmarks below count hard sets only.

Rough RPE to %1RM mapping for a 5-rep set: RPE 7 ≈ 75–78%, RPE 8 ≈ 80–82%, RPE 9 ≈ 85–87%. Use RPE for prescription and percentages for planning blocks.

## Weekly volume landmarks (hard sets per muscle group per week)

| Purpose | Hard sets / muscle / week | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Maintenance | 6–10 | Roughly one third of what built the muscle, as long as intensity stays at RPE 7–9 |
| Productive growth | 12–20 | Build from the bottom to the top of the range across a block |
| Advanced growth | up to 22 | Only for lifters with years of consistent training and recovery to match |
| Brief overreach | 22–28 | One to two weeks at most, followed immediately by a deload |
| Beyond 28 | Rarely productive | Rising injury risk, flat returns |

In a fat-loss deficit, hold 10–15 sets per muscle: enough to preserve, not so much that recovery collapses on fewer calories.

## Deload prescription

One week at **50–60% of normal volume** and **about 70% of normal intensity**. Keep the movements the same so the skill stays sharp; just do less of them.

Default cadence, unless fatigue arrives sooner:

| Training level | Deload every |
|---|---|
| Beginner | 8–12 weeks, or when linear progression stalls twice on multiple lifts |
| Intermediate | 4–6 weeks |
| Advanced / elite | 3–5 weeks, planned in advance |

Deload *before* the crash, not after. Signs it is overdue: RPE climbing 2+ points at the same load, joint achiness across several sessions, sleep degrading, motivation dropping, the client saying "I feel beat up."

## Protein targets (g per kg bodyweight per day)

| Situation | Target |
|---|---|
| General health / maintenance | 1.4–1.8 |
| Hypertrophy (surplus) | 1.6–2.2 |
| Fat loss or body recomposition (deficit or maintenance) | 2.0–2.4 |
| Older adults (60+), especially while cutting | 2.0–2.4 (anabolic resistance raises the floor) |
| Elite athletes in strength/hypertrophy blocks | 2.0–2.4 |

Spread across 3–5 meals, roughly 0.4 g/kg per meal. Protein is the one macro that stays fixed when calories move.

## Calorie anchors

These are coaching anchors, not a nutrition prescription. Refer to a registered dietitian or a nutrition-coach skill for precise macros.

- **Maintenance estimate**: bodyweight (kg) × 30–35 kcal for moderate activity. Confirm against the scale over 2–3 weeks.
- **Hypertrophy surplus**: +200–300 kcal/day. Larger surpluses add fat faster than muscle.
- **Standard fat-loss deficit**: 15–20% below maintenance, targeting 0.5–1.0% bodyweight lost per week.
- **Recomp**: maintenance to 10–15% deficit depending on training age (beginners tolerate the deficit; advanced lifters need maintenance).
- **Breastfeeding**: cap the deficit at 250–300 kcal/day to protect milk supply.
- **Older adults**: cut at 0.25–0.5% bodyweight per week, slower than younger clients.

## Cardio zones

| Zone | Feel | Approx. % max HR | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Zone 2 | Conversational; full sentences | 60–70% | Aerobic base, recovery, health. Low recovery cost. |
| Zone 4 | Hard; short phrases only | 80–90% | Intervals, 4–6 min work / 2–3 min easy |
| Zone 5 | Maximal; cannot talk | 90%+ | Short intervals, sparingly |

Default weekly mix unless a specialty says otherwise: 1–2 Zone 2 sessions plus 0–1 interval session. Cap high-intensity intervals at 2 sessions per week; beyond that they start to cost lifting recovery. Daily walking (8,000–12,000 steps) adds to energy expenditure at essentially zero recovery cost.

The old "keep heart rate under 140 bpm in pregnancy" rule is outdated. Use RPE and the talk test instead.

## Resistance / cardio split by goal

| Goal | Resistance / Cardio |
|---|---|
| Pure hypertrophy | 80 / 20 |
| Body recomp | 65 / 35 |
| Fat loss | 55 / 45 |
| Maintenance | 60 / 40 |
| Functional / general health | 50 / 50 |
| Endurance sport | 30 / 70 |

Within a session, lift before cardio unless the goal is sport-specific endurance. If both happen the same day in separate sessions, leave 4–6 hours between them.

## Progressive overload models

| Model | Who | How |
|---|---|---|
| Linear progression | Beginners | Add weight every successful session: +5 lb on squat/bench/press, +10 lb on deadlift, smallest jump on dumbbells. Two failures in a row → drop 10% and ramp back. |
| Double progression | Intermediates | Hit the top of the rep range on all sets at RPE ≤ 8 → add weight, drop to the bottom of the range, rebuild. |
| Undulating (DUP) | Intermediate–advanced | Heavy / light / medium days within the week, or week-to-week waves. |
| Block / percentage-based | Advanced–elite | 4–8 week blocks at prescribed %1RM with a defined emphasis (hypertrophy → strength → peak). |

## Pain scale for training decisions

Used by `injury-prep`, `post-knee-surgery`, and `form-correction`. Not a diagnosis.

| Pain during a movement | Action |
|---|---|
| 0–3 / 10 | Workable. Continue and monitor. |
| 4–6 / 10 | Regress the movement (lighter load, shorter range, more stable variation). |
| 7+ / 10 | Remove the movement for now. |
| Sharp, radiating, or worsening | Stop. Refer to a physician or physical therapist before continuing. |

## Strength benchmarks for "advanced"

Used by `elite-athlete` to confirm the label, and by `program-design` to choose defaults.

| Lift | Men | Women |
|---|---|---|
| Squat | ≥ 2.0× bodyweight | ≥ 1.5× bodyweight |
| Bench | ≥ 1.5× bodyweight | ≥ 1.0× bodyweight |
| Deadlift | ≥ 2.5× bodyweight | ≥ 2.0× bodyweight |

Plus 4+ years of consistent training or competitive standing in a strength sport. Meeting one benchmark does not make someone advanced; the picture does.
